Linking models to data

Assessing the “closeness” of model output and data

Probabilistic formulation

  • Often we know something about how the data were taken, so observations introduce uncertainty.

  • We can express the uncertainty in observing the process as a probability

    p(data∣underlying process)p(\text{data} \mid \text{underlying process})

  • Including this in our model gives

    p(data∣model output)p(\text{data} \mid \text{model output})

Probability distributions: discrete

How many soldiers die of horse kicks, at 0.61 deaths per corps per year? Counts of rare, independent events follow a Poisson distribution.

From one point to the whole trajectory

Multiply across the data to get the probability of the whole trajectory:

p(data∣θ)=∏ip(data point i∣θ)p(\text{data} \mid \theta) = \prod_{i} p(\text{data point } i \mid \theta)

Or sum on the log scale, which is numerically better behaved:

log⁡p(data∣θ)=∑ilog⁡p(data point i∣θ)\log p(\text{data} \mid \theta) = \sum_{i} \log p(\text{data point } i \mid \theta)

The likelihood

  • We compare models to data using probabilities:

    p(data∣model output)p(\text{data} \mid \text{model output})

  • For a given model this depends on the parameters θ\theta:

    L(θ)=p(data∣θ)L(\theta) = p(\text{data} \mid \theta)

    is called the likelihood of parameters θ\theta. Here θ\theta encompasses all parameters, e.g. θ={β,γ}\theta = \{\beta, \gamma\}.

  • Likelihoods can span a wide range of orders of magnitude, which causes numerical problems. Taking the logarithm gives the log-likelihood.

Bayesian inference

Two rules of probability

Two general rules, true of any joint distribution, are all we need to get p(θ∣data)p(\theta \mid \text{data}).

  • Conditional probability: p(A∣B)=p(A,B)p(B)p(A \mid B) = \dfrac{p(A,B)}{p(B)}, rearranged into the chain rule, two ways to factorise a joint distribution:

    p(A,B)=p(A∣B)p(B)=p(B∣A)p(A)p(A,B) = p(A \mid B)\,p(B) = p(B \mid A)\,p(A)

  • Marginalisation: integrating (or summing) a variable out of a joint distribution to get the distribution of the rest:

    p(B)=∫p(A,B)dAp(B) = \int p(A,B)\,\mathrm{d}A

We’ll meet marginalisation again for the particle filter likelihood.

Bayes’ rule

The chain rule applied to θ\theta and the data gives two factorisations of p(θ,data)p(\theta, \text{data}) that must be equal. Dividing through by p(data)p(\text{data}) gives:

p(θ∣data)=p(data∣θ)p(θ)p(data)p(\theta \mid \text{data}) = \frac{p(\text{data} \mid \theta)\, p(\theta)}{p(\text{data})}

  • p(θ∣data)p(\theta \mid \text{data}) is the posterior
  • p(data∣θ)p(\text{data} \mid \theta) is the likelihood
  • p(θ)p(\theta) is the prior
  • p(data)p(\text{data}) is a normalisation constant: θ\theta marginalised out of p(θ,data)p(\theta, \text{data}), i.e. ∫p(data∣θ)p(θ)dθ\int p(\text{data} \mid \theta)\, p(\theta) \, \mathrm{d}\theta

In words,

(posterior)∝(normalised likelihood)×(prior)\text{(posterior)} \propto \text{(normalised likelihood)} \times \text{(prior)}

Prior probabilities

p(θ)p(\theta) is our belief about the parameters before we see the data, taken from previous measurements, the literature, or experts.

A prior for the R0R_0 of measles

Example: estimating R0R_0 of measles

Checking a prior by simulating from it

A prior on R0R_0 is hard to have intuition about. An epidemic curve is not.

So draw parameters from the prior, run the model, and look at what you get. This is a prior predictive simulation, and it turns an abstract claim into a picture you can judge.

If your prior produces epidemics that infect the whole population in four days, you have learned something you could not have seen from the parameter range.

Sampling from the posterior

Parameters θ\theta are interpreted as a random variable, distributed according to the posterior:

p(θ∣data)∝p(data∣θ)p(θ)p(\theta \mid \text{data}) \propto p(\text{data} \mid \theta)\, p(\theta)

We want to generate samples of θ\theta from this distribution.

We cannot just try all parameter combinations

You can explore a two-parameter posterior on a grid. Fifteen values each is 225 model runs, which is nothing.
